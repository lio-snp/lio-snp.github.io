0. Two reference points

The same model checkpoints and texts give two different views of representation change:

  • Cosine similarity between the final-layer representations and step 0, the randomly initialized model, is −0.003 near the end of training: the directions are almost orthogonal.
  • Cosine similarity to the preceding checkpoint has a median of +0.993, with only one interval below 0.9 across training.

The first comparison measures alignment with a fixed starting point; the second measures alignment over a local interval. A representation can move far from initialization through a sequence of small changes. Interpreting either curve therefore requires specifying both the reference point and the metric.

TL;DR

  1. I started out hoping that one curve would tell me whether the model had stopped changing. Then I realized I had changed two things at once.
  2. I thought the small late-training changes might just reflect a learning rate that hadn't reached zero. Looking closer, drift speed was correlated with the learning rate, but an approximately 22-fold jump near 116k needed a separate look (Section 4.3).
  3. Along the way, I found problems in the metric implementation and checkpoint labels. Section 7 records that debugging process.

1. Background and question

Two lines of work motivate a closer look at how representation change is measured:

  • Mechanisms: interpretable circuits can emerge during training. Induction heads, for example, help copy patterns from earlier context, and their emergence is associated with changes in in-context learning.
  • Evaluation: an abrupt-looking ability curve can depend on the scoring rule. In some evaluations across model scales, a continuous metric reveals a smoother change than a discrete score.

These concern different objects: the formation of mechanisms during training and the measurement of abilities across scales. Both make the choice of observable relevant. Here the question is narrower: across Pythia's 154 checkpoints, do representations drift continuously, or are there intervals with unusually large changes?

Terminology

  • Checkpoint: a saved model at a particular training step. Pythia releases 154 checkpoints, from step 0 (random initialization) to step 143000, available as Hugging Face revisions.
  • Activation: a numerical response of a neuron to an input.
  • Hidden state: an internal representation vector, with 2048 coordinates in this model. A projection can display some of its variation in two dimensions.
  • Orthogonal: vectors at a 90° angle have cosine similarity 0. In 2048 dimensions, independent isotropic random directions already tend to have cosine similarity near 0. A value near zero indicates little directional alignment; its significance depends on the comparison.

2. Setup

The analysis uses Pythia-1B and its 154 intermediate checkpoints from a single training run with a consistent recipe and data.

The probe set contains 126 hand-written texts: 9 categories (punctuation, numbers/dates, syntax, mathematics, code, few-shot ICL, names, sentiment, and definitions) × Chinese and English × 7 examples per combination. This mixture allows the same neuron's responses to be compared across several input types and stages of training.

At each checkpoint, a forward pass records mean-pooled and last-token hidden states at 5 sampled layers (0/4/8/12/15), peak activations for each MLP neuron and the tokens that triggered them, and loss on the probe texts.

Processing took about 4.7 hours on two GPUs. Each revision's weights (4 GB) were downloaded, used for the forward pass, and deleted after the derived statistics were saved.

3. Loss and representation change

On these 126 texts, loss falls from 10.98 → 3.40 (bottom-right panel). A uniform prediction over Pythia's vocabulary of 50304 tokens gives a loss of 10.83, providing a baseline for the initial value.

The decrease is not monotonic: loss rises in 56 of the 153 adjacent intervals, with the largest increase at step 90000 (+0.55). Over the same training run, the final layer's cosine similarity to step 0 falls from 1.00 to −0.003 (top-left panel).

Loss measures predictive performance on the probe texts, whereas cosine measures alignment between representations. Their overall trends do not establish whether representations have stopped changing late in training. That requires a comparison between later checkpoints.

4. Reference points, intervals, and learning rate

Three comparisons separate distance from initialization, local displacement, and relationships among samples. The first two are examined here; sample relationships are considered with CKA in Section 5.

4.1 Alignment with initialization

The layer-wise profiles start at 1.0 and decline sharply over the first few dozen steps. For layer 15:

The sharpest change among these values is step 8 to 16 (0.906 → 0.300). This falls within warmup, when Pythia's learning rate rises linearly from 0 to 2.5e-4 over 1430 steps. The early changes therefore occur under an increasing learning rate.

A quantity derived from this initialization-relative comparison was initially described as “82.8% of the drift completed by step 100.” The selected checkpoint was actually step 128 (see Section 7). More fundamentally, the quantity is nonmonotonic: it reaches 87.9% at step 32 and falls to 82.3% at step 64. It cannot be interpreted as a fraction of completed change.

4.2 Displacement between adjacent checkpoints

To measure local change, displacement between neighboring checkpoints is divided by the number of training steps in the interval. For layer 15:

The previous-checkpoint reference moves with the model, while step 0 remains fixed. High local alignment can therefore coexist with low alignment to initialization. This comparison avoids changing the reference point and metric simultaneously.

Net displacement per step is not path length per step. Movement within an interval can cancel, so early 8-step intervals and late 1000-step intervals have different temporal resolution even after division by step count.

4.3 The learning-rate trend and the event near 116k

A shrinking displacement could partly reflect a shrinking update scale. The reconstructed Pythia schedule has a peak learning rate of 2.5e-4, cosine decay to 2.5e-5, and 1430 warmup steps.

Over the fitted interval, log(speed) and log(learning rate) are correlated, with R² = 0.58–0.76. This association does not identify the learning rate as the cause of the trend or explain every fluctuation. The interval near 116k departs from the smooth schedule:

Between step 115k and 116k, layer 15's displacement per step rises from 0.0783 to 1.7007, about 22-fold. Two consecutive intervals (115k→116k and 116k→117k) have values of this order, while the learning rate decreases smoothly. The tail median (≥133k) is 0.0756; the event is 11.8 times that value.

The five Hugging Face weight files for step114000–118000 have different sha256 hashes. They contain distinct data, although this check alone does not exclude loading or numerical-precision issues. The representation measurements show:

  • Coherent displacement directions: the 126 probe texts move in nearly the same direction. In the middle layers, cos(individual displacement, mean displacement) = +0.99, compared with 0.58 in the ordinary interval 133k→134k.
  • Adjacent-checkpoint cosine similarity drops from 0.998 → 0.184 and then rebounds (panel (c)). Last-token similarity also changes (0.997 → 0.339), with changes across the sampled layers.
  • After subtracting the respective means and projecting out the common direction, cosine similarity remains around 0.6. A difference remains, but this does not establish a rotation.

Mean loss changes by +0.013 nats on the current probe set, with changes in both directions across categories (code −0.053, names +0.117). The event is therefore much more visible in representation displacement than in mean loss on these texts. Its cause and effects on other behaviors remain unresolved.

5. Sample relationships under CKA

Cosine compares vector directions. CKA (Centered Kernel Alignment) instead compares the similarity structure among samples in two representation matrices. This asks whether texts that have related representations in one checkpoint remain related in another. In the 154×154 heatmap:

  • Step 0 has dark comparisons with trained checkpoints. Its sample-similarity structure differs from theirs under this metric.
  • Late checkpoints are mostly bright when compared with one another (CKA ≈ 1), indicating similar sample relationships on the probe set.
  • Relative to step 0, layer 8's CKA decreases from 1.0 to 0.134; the final layer ends at 0.321.

The final layer thus has cosine similarity ≈ 0 to step 0 but CKA around 0.32. This cannot be explained as a pure rotation: linear CKA is 1 under an orthogonal transformation. An intermediate value also needs a baseline before it can be interpreted.

The shuffled control keeps the representations fixed and breaks the correspondence between rows and texts:

ComparisonValue
Observed CKA (step 0, final)0.321
100 shuffles0.095 ± 0.009 (maximum 0.118)

The observed value exceeds the shuffled distribution, supporting sample correspondence beyond this baseline. It does not mean “32% of the structure was preserved” or that similarity rankings are unchanged. Directional alignment and sample relationships describe different aspects of the representations.

Linear separability of category and language

Linear probes fit logistic regression with 5-fold cross-validation on one layer's hidden states, using the same protocol for two classification tasks:

TaskProbe accuracy ↑
Step 0Final

Before training, language accuracy is already 0.857. Category accuracy of 0.397 also exceeds the 9-class chance level of 0.111 (3.6 times higher). Initialization therefore contains signals usable by these classifiers. Training raises category accuracy from 39.7% to 79.3% under the same protocol.

Category accuracy does not isolate semantic understanding: vocabulary, punctuation, length, or templates may distinguish the classes. Comparing the speed at which language and category signals emerge would require a separate fit.

The 9 category drift curves also nearly overlap (bottom-right panel in Section 3). Within the probe set, representation drift is shared across categories rather than confined to one input type.

7. Implementation corrections

Three implementation errors affected earlier versions of the analysis. The corrected measurements above use the revised implementation.

CKA input dimensions. The function expected [checkpoints, samples, features], but the 5 sampled layers had been placed on the last axis. This represented each sample with 5 features and produced an incorrect heatmap.

CKA normalization. Matrix multiplication had been used in place of the Frobenius inner product sum(G_i ∘ G_j). The resulting self-similarity values were not 1. Correcting the normalization exposed the remaining dark bands caused by the input-dimension error.

Checkpoint labels. The summary script used argmin|step - 100|, selected step 128, and printed “100.” The earlier “82.8% completed by step 100” statement combined this labeling error with the nonmonotonic quantity discussed in Section 4.1.

8. Limitations

  • The 126 hand-written texts do not establish generalization to broader corpora or millions of tokens.
  • Only 5 / 16 layers were sampled.
  • Max-activating exemplars come from text fragments retained before the model crashed, rather than a large-scale corpus scan.
  • The finite checkpoint sequence does not establish eventual convergence. Pythia's learning rate remains nonzero; setting it to 0 would stop weight updates by construction. A separate intervention would be needed to distinguish the learning-rate trend from other sources of representation change.
  • The 116k event's cause is unresolved. Data-order boundaries, precision, and optimizer state remain possible factors to investigate against the training configuration and full weights. The current loss measurements do not characterize its effects on other behaviors.

9. Open questions

The main unresolved observation is the roughly 22-fold displacement jump near 116k with a small change in mean probe loss. Comparisons across Pythia scales (70M to 12B) could test whether similar events occur elsewhere and whether their frequency varies with model size.

A few hundred thousand tokens from the Pile would also provide broader input coverage than the current 126 texts. These extensions would separate questions about this probe set from questions about the training run.

Measurement summary

Distance from initialization describes alignment with a fixed reference; adjacent displacement describes change over an observed interval; CKA describes sample relationships. None alone determines whether the model has stopped changing. In these measurements, the learning-rate trend accompanies an overall slowdown, while the event near 116k remains a distinct observation with an unresolved cause.
